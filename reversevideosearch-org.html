Reverse Video Search: Finding Where a Clip Came From

By Xinyue Zhan, Content Lead at Pavo AI

Reverse image search is a familiar habit. Reverse video search is less common, mostly because video is harder: frames, audio and edits all change the file. The practical approach is to extract representative frames and search those, which is what a dedicated tool can automate.

The use cases are mundane but real. You see a clip in a feed and want the original upload. You inherit a folder of stock footage with no attribution file. You want to check whether a video you are about to license has already been published elsewhere. Doing this by hand means screenshots and several search tabs; a purpose-built tool collapses that into one step.

Reverse Video Search is at reversevideosearch.org . It is worth keeping bookmarked if you regularly handle footage of uncertain origin, and it pairs well with a frame-extraction step for clips where the opening seconds are not representative.
