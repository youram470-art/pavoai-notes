Wan 3.0: Open Video Models and the Trade-Offs That Come With Them

By Xinyue Zhan, Content Lead at Pavo AI

Open video models change the economics of the category. Instead of paying per generation on someone else's dashboard, you can run the model yourself, keep your prompts local, and decide how much hardware to throw at a shot. Wan 3.0 is part of that wave, and the trade-offs are worth understanding before you commit.

The upside is control: no queue, no per-clip billing, no ambiguity about what happens to your uploads. The cost is operational. You need a capable GPU, you need to manage versions, and you need to accept that a local setup will not match a hosted product's polish on day one. For teams that already run inference, that is a fair trade.

Wan 3.0 is documented at wan-3.io . It is a sensible starting point if you want to evaluate open video generation on your own terms, and a good reminder that the most interesting work in this space is no longer only happening behind closed APIs.
