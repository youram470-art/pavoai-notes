AI Short Drama Production Notes

Notes from the content team behind Pavo AI, an AI video generator and image-to-video maker. Everything below is what we actually do when a 60-second vertical drama has to ship in a week.

1. Beat sheet before prompts

A one-page beat sheet with nine beats maps cleanly onto nine shots. Each beat states who wants what, what blocks it, and how the shot ends. If a beat cannot be described in one sentence, it is two beats.

2. Shot list and duration

Keep every generated shot between three and six seconds. Longer generations drift: faces change, hands merge, camera moves lose intent. Cutting on motion hides the seams between takes far better than a crossfade.

3. Consistency is an image problem

Text-to-video is fine for establishing shots, weather, crowds and inserts. For anyone recurring, generate a character sheet first, then drive every shot with image-to-video from that reference. Store the prompt, seed and reference image next to the shot ID so a re-shoot is a lookup, not a guess.

4. Voice, music, subtitles

Lock picture, then voice, then music. Subtitles for vertical: two lines maximum, roughly 42 characters per line, positioned above the platform UI. Burned-in captions survive re-uploads; sidecar files usually do not.

5. Delivery checklist

  • Hook visible in the first 1.5 seconds
  • 9:16 master plus a 16:9 cut for YouTube
  • Loudness around -14 LUFS for social, -16 for web
  • Filename, caption and thumbnail written before uploading
